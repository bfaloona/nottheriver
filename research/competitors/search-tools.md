# Search tools, apps, extensions and local-shop finders that steer shoppers to ethical, sustainable or independent sellers

Status: complete for this pass. All pages were read on 2026-10-09 unless a line says otherwise. Scope: tools with a search box, a scanner, a map or a marketplace filter. Directories, rating sites and certification finders are in `directories.md` (sibling agent); where one of those also ships an app or extension it gets one line here. The 26 Amazon-alternative list sites in `research/sites/` (checked 2026-09-25, index `research/index.json`) are cited, not redone: Buycott, IndieBound, Amazon Brand Detector and DoneGood already have profiles there.

How to read this file:

- Every claim about a tool comes from a page named in section D with the date read. "Not stated" means the pages read do not say. Nothing is from memory.
- Feature labels FT1 to FT14 are the ones in the operator's brief (listed under B). A cell is "yes" only when a page read this run shows the feature; "partial" when part of it is there or it is there for brands rather than shops; "no" when it was not found on the pages read, which is not proof it does not exist.
- Distances are in miles. Prices are as the page shows them.

### A1. Boycat (boycat.io)

- Owner and funding: company not named ("© 2026 Boycat"; the FAQ says the team "stay[s] private for safety"). "Self-funded" and "community-built" per the FAQ; membership "from $1/mo" ("We don't sell data. We don't run ads. Membership is how we stay independent"); paid VPN plans; a "Buycat marketplace" with "exclusive discounts" (affiliate status not stated); BuycatFinds links to Amazon (commission not stated).
- What it is: iOS and Android app, browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Brave, Opera), website.
- Scope: global ("free worldwide"); brands and products; Amazon product pages are screened in the extension.
- Data source: "Targeted boycott campaigns backed by research"; community contribution and verification process not described on the pages read. Purrsuit (a sister product) cites "public FEC and IRS filings".
- Ranking and explanation: flagged products show "the violated campaign, the reason, and alternatives" (Chrome listing). No score, no components, no source link on the pages read. BuycatFinds names a "top pick" citing review sites, with no stated method.
- Negatives: yes, as campaign flags with a reason; sources and dates for individual flags not shown on the pages read.
- Disputes: not stated (FAQ has "Campaigns & Ethics" with no answers; support link only).
- Local: none; nothing about nearby shops.
- Privacy: "Compliance checks send an anonymous request containing only the domain name or brand identifier"; browsing and purchases "not logged"; sign-in stores email and tier; analytics "feature-level only and off by default"; VPN logs country and session length. Privacy policy page returned 404.
- Affiliate or sponsorship: no ads; affiliate status of marketplace and Amazon links not stated.
- Open source: not stated.
- Last visible update: extension 4.0.1, 2026-05-31 (ChromeBoard); blog posts to 2026-02.
- FT: FT1 partial, FT3 partial, FT4 partial, FT8 partial, FT11 partial, FT13 partial.

### A3. Goodbuy (trygoodbuy.com)

- Owner and funding: goodbuy, Inc. (App Store). Consumer app "free to download and use. No subscription or fees" (App Store). The 2023 Modern Retail interview says it charged no fee in year one and "brands will pay Goodbuy a cut of the sale if a shopper used the platform to discover a product". The site today sells a "white-label marketplace" to credit unions ("The engine for small business account growth", SOC 2 Type II, Finovate Fall 2026). Pricing not stated.
- What it is: Chrome and Safari extension plus iOS app that shows small-business alternatives while you browse "mega-retailer sites"; the app also has its own search.
- Scope: US small businesses ("Discover 185k small businesses"); products and shops.
- Data source: "some third-party data" plus proprietary tools to find businesses on Shopify and other platforms; businesses self-state their scale and values at onboarding (Modern Retail, 2023-01-19).
- Ranking and explanation: not explained; the App Store listing says alternatives are suggested but not how they are matched.
- Negatives: none.
- Disputes: not stated.
- Local: filter values include "local"; the credit-union site says members "Discover local businesses". No map or distance seen.
- Privacy: App Store label links name, email and user ID to the user; "goodbuy does not sell your personal information". The credit-union site cites GDPR and California compliance.
- Affiliate or sponsorship: affiliate model stated as the plan in 2023; the Shopify app listing (which search snippets describe as an affiliate network) now reads "This app is not currently available on the Shopify App Store".
- Open source: not stated.
- Last visible update: iOS app 1.0.23, 2023-08-03; Chrome listing did not render (title "empty-title"); the website is dated 2026 but is B2B.
- Status: the consumer product looks dormant; not confirmed.
- FT: FT1 partial, FT2 partial, FT6 partial, FT7 partial, FT12 partial, FT13 partial.

### C2. Features that are common

- FT1 product search exists at the big marketplaces and Google, and in a product-to-local-stock form at Locally. Among ethics tools it is rare: most look up a brand or scan a barcode (Buycott, Shop Ethical!, Goods Unite Us) or react to the page you are on (Boycat, Goodbuy, Beagle, Progressive Shopper).
- FT2 nearby shops on a map exists in general-purpose tools (Google, Yelp, Amex, chamber apps, Lokjo) with no ethics data, and in ethics tools not at all. Nobody combines FT2 with FT3 or FT4.
- FT13 "show me an alternative" is the standard move of the extension category (6 partials), always triggered on Amazon's page and usually paid by affiliate commission on the alternative.

### C4. Where their shop data comes from, and what it implies for the open-place-data direction

| Source | Tools using it | Implication for `docs/feasibility-overview.md` |
|---|---|---|
| OpenStreetMap | Lokjo | A working worldwide local-shop map runs on OSM with no ads and no tracking; it shows the data is usable for FT2, and it does not attempt "sells it" or independence, which is where nottheriver's category and chain work would add value. Whether OSM shop coverage and category tags are good enough for US "sells it" lookups is the coverage check already planned. |
| Merchant self-declaration | Google small business, Yelp attributes, Goodbuy onboarding, Etsy | Self-declared "small" or "local" is cheap and scales but is unsourced; nottheriver's sourced chain list is the opposite trade. A self-declared field could be a secondary signal, never a badge. |
| Membership rolls | Bookshop.org and IndieBound (ABA), MyChamberApp (chambers), Amex (card acceptance) | Curated directories with one external gate each; none carries sources or dispute state. A directory built from such rolls would inherit their gate (for example ABA membership) and should cite it as the source. |
| Retailer POS feeds | Locally | The only "in stock" signal found; it costs brands money and sells shopper location. Out of reach for a no-funding project, and in conflict with FT8. |
| Crowd and campaign data | Buycott, Boycat | Scales fast, unsourced, undated; the model nottheriver's accepted-source list is designed to avoid. |

### C5. Funding models seen and what they imply for FT11 and FT14

| Model | Tools | FT11 | FT14 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliate commission on the alternative | Bookshop.org, Thingtesting, Beagle, Goodbuy (planned), Progressive Shopper, Ecosia shop links, Good On You | Fails FT11 by design; the incentive points at the alternative that pays. Beagle's "we never accept money to show products" shows the category knows the objection. | No budget ceiling: more clicks mean more money, so degradation never arises. |
| Advertising | Google, Yelp, Etsy, eBay, DuckDuckGo, Brave, Goods Unite Us | Fails FT11. | Same. |
| Brand-paid placement and data | Locally (co-op dollars, location sold), Finch's old plan (data to brands) | Fails FT11 and FT8 together. | Same. |
| Membership or subscription | Boycat ($1/mo), Finch, Shop Ethical! (Patreon), Goods Unite Us premium | Compatible with FT11; Boycat and Finch both state "no ads". | A paid tier is the standard answer to cost; nottheriver's FT14 (say when the free budget is spent) has no precedent here. |
| Non-profit with donations | Lokjo, Shop Ethical!, (The Markup) | Compatible with FT11. | Nobody publishes a cost ledger or a per-search cost; FT9 and FT14 would be new in this scope. |

Two things follow. First, FT11 is a genuine differentiator only against the extension and marketplace category; the non-profit tools already meet it, so the claim should be paired with FT9 (show the cost) to be distinct. Second, no tool in this scope tells users when it is degraded for money reasons; FT14 has no competitor and no user expectation to meet, which cuts both ways (nothing to copy, nothing to be measured against).
