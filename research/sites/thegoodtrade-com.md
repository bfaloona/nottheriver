"15+ Ethical Shopping Alternatives To Amazon (2026)", bylined "Our Editors", published 2026-03-19 and updated 2026-08-26. The Good Trade is a Los Angeles lifestyle publication that calls itself "an independently-owned media brand whose work is primarily reader-supported through affiliate links"; its about page names a co-founder and CEO. The article opens with reasons to leave Amazon (plastic packaging waste, delivery emissions, worker injuries "at twice the industry rate", linked to a journal paper and The Verge), then states three inclusion criteria (sustainable and ethical practices, clean and safe products, a preference for small businesses) and gives a "Best for" category, certifications and shipping regions for each pick. A commission disclosure sits at the top, under the headline. The first eight picks all use affiliate links, and #2 (Shop) carries a "The Good Trade Partner" label that the methodology page doesn't explain. Plain links cluster lower in the list.

## Score
- Independence (13/20): commission disclosure directly under the headline, and the owner is named; deducted because #2 is labeled "The Good Trade Partner" (likely paid placement, not explained on the methodology page) and 11 of 19 links are affiliate-wrapped.

- Substance (15/20): 19 real, non-Amazon options with a specific reason each, across groceries, household, beauty, books, gifts, secondhand and tech; deducted because 4 are UK-only and 2 are free-exchange networks rather than shops. After calibration 17 rows count as shops; the two free-exchange networks are under "Also named".
- Currency (13/15): updated 2026-08-26; retailers' current operation not checked this run beyond three affiliate links resolved to grove.co, uncommongoods.com and ebay.com pages.
- Usefulness (14/15): organized by "Best for" product type, lists where each retailer ships, UK picks in their own section.
- No dark patterns (10/10): no Amazon or Amazon-owned links, no bait headline, no repeated top pick; the "Partner" pick at #2 is scored under independence only, and the newsletter prompt is not a wall.
