Search

Find who sells it near you or online, ranked by how they treat people and the planet, never Amazon.

Type a product and a US zip code. You get shops near you and online retailers that sell it or something close, each ranked with its reasons shown. There are no accounts, cookies, or analytics.

Why Amazon never appears

Amazon, and every business it owns or has owned, never appears in results at any rank. That includes Whole Foods Market, Zappos, Ring, and Audible. Code removes them twice: once right after the web search, and again as the last step before results reach your browser. The language model is never the only check.

Every entry on the exclusion list cites a public source: data/blocklist.md.

How ranking works

Each result gets four scores between 0 and 1, and its rank comes from their weighted sum. The weights are listed above every set of results, and "Why this rank" on each result says in plain words what went into its score, linking each certification or concern to its source.

  • Relevance: 1 when the shop's listing names the product or a close equivalent, 0.5 when it matches only the product's category, otherwise 0.2. For a shop near you, the language model's judgment from the shop's name and type also counts: 1 if it judged the shop likely sells the product, 0.5 if maybe.
  • Ethics: starts at 0.5. Each ethics certification (B Corp, Fair Trade, worker co-op) adds 0.25, up to 1. Each accepted labor or governance finding subtracts 0.25, down to 0.
  • Environment: starts at 0.5. Each environmental certification (1% for the Planet, Climate Neutral) adds 0.25, up to 1. Each accepted environmental finding subtracts 0.25, down to 0.
  • Proximity: for shops near you, 1 at the center of your zip area, falling to 0 at 40 km (about 25 miles). Online retailers get 0.5.

"Baseline 0.5" in a score's sources means that score started at 0.5 and nothing changed it.

Certifications come from a short curated list in which every entry cites its source. A negative finding lowers a score only when its source is on the list of accepted sources: regulators, court records, established news organizations, and recognized labor and environmental watchdogs. The language model suggests search terms, judges whether each result is a shop that sells the product and leaves out those it judges are not, and points to findings in the search results; the text shown for a finding is always the source's own title or snippet, never words the model wrote. Details: docs/ranking.md.

Disputing a ranking

Anyone can dispute a certification, a negative finding, or any part of a score, including the business itself. Every negative finding shown on a result has a "Dispute this" link that leads here or to the dispute form. How disputes are reviewed and resolved: TBD.

Open a dispute with the dispute issue template. It asks for the retailer, the signal shown, the source link, and evidence for the correction.

Privacy

Your zip code never leaves your browser. The site looks it up in a file it downloads and sends only the product, the city and state, the center of your zip area rounded to two decimal places, and its rural-urban category (used only to decide how far counts as nearby). The search service uses that center to find nearby shops, so it learns the zip area of each search; the language model sees only the product, city, and state. The site never asks for a street address. The map of nearby shops loads its images from OpenStreetMap, so OpenStreetMap sees your IP address and roughly where the search was. The rate limiter keeps your IP address for its one-minute window, and the hosting platforms keep request logs under their own policies. Details: docs/privacy.md.

Coverage and quality

Results come from Brave Search's index. Shops that block search crawlers, or that the index has not reached, may be missing. Quality is measured on a fixed set of test searches: how many results really sell the product (precision), and how many good shops found by an independent search engine this site also found (recall).
