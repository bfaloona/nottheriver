WF2 Place: low-fidelity wireframe

Lens: where the visitor is and where things are. Every state is drawn twice as a static frame, 360 px and 1280 px wide. Underlined text is a link. Boxes like [x] are drawn controls. Shops, chains and sources are invented. Scores, per-search counts, token counts and dates on example shops are placeholders; scores follow the four weights in the inventory. Accuracy, cost and time figures are the inventory's.

How the design is built. One drawing, called the plan, is the frame for everything: a mark for the center of the visitor's zip area, a circle for the nearby distance (10 or 30 miles), a circle for 100 miles, and a strip off the plan for things with no place (online shops, and nearby shops whose position is not listed). The plan is drawn in the browser from the zip, with no map service and no outside request. Results are grouped by zone, nearest zone first; the number on each mark is the shop's place in the one overall order by score. Trust, privacy and cost are reached from the plan's legend and are themselves arranged by place: accuracy per zone, and a path of the places a search travels to. The list under the plan repeats everything on the plan as text, in the same order, for keyboard and screen reader use.

Privacy and cost are one drawing of places: what stays in the browser, what reaches the site's service, and which outside parties it is passed to. Reached from the zip field before anything is typed, and from the legend after a search (then it also shows that search's cost).
9. Privacy and cost, 360 px
Back to the planWhere your search goes

Where your search goes, and what it costs

Place 1: your browser. Stays here.
  • Your zip. Never sent, never kept, never in the page address.
  • The last product you typed, until this tab closes.
  • The plan itself: drawn here from the zip.
Place 2: this site's service. Sent, used once.
  • The product; your city and state; the center of your zip area (rounded to about 0.6 mile); one of 10 rural-to-urban categories.
  • The search as a whole, the shops found and the scores are not stored.
  • Your IP address (for IPv6, its first half): held one minute by the rate limiter.
  • The log line says only worked or not, and how long. Not stored.
  • Kept 30 days: how the product was read, shared by everyone who types the same product. In effect a list of products people searched for, with no place and no IP address.
Place 3: outside parties. Passed on, under each one's own policy.
  • Brave Search: search wording made from the product, and the center of your zip area. It learns the zip area of each search. Not your IP address.
  • OpenRouter and the model provider it routes to: the product text, then titles and excerpts of results, which can name nearby towns. No location fields.
  • Cloudflare and GitHub Pages (hosts): ordinary request records, including IP address.
Nowhere else. No accounts, cookies, analytics or tracking. The page loads nothing from any other party: no map service, no outside fonts. A shop learns nothing unless you follow its link, and then it is not told where you came from.
What this search cost: 5 web lookups, 9,100 language-model tokens, about $0.023.
