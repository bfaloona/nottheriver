WF1: the decision as a sequence in time

Low-fidelity wireframe. One page is one path of six steps: 1 What, 2 Where, 3 Search, 4 How soon, 5 Which shop, 6 Go. Finished steps fold to one line, the current step is open, and each step carries the questions that arise at that moment ("Asked at this step"). Shops and sources are invented and say "Example". Score values are example values. Underlined text stands for a link; nothing here loads anything.

Before step 1

What is this for?

Finding who sells a product, near you or online, ordered by how the sellers treat people and the planet.

Why is there no Amazon, and what counts?

Leaving it out is the reason the site exists. Amazon and every business it owns or has owned: 39 web domains and 14 business names, each with a public source and date. Code removes them twice per search; the language model is never the only check. The exclusion set

Who pays, and is anyone paying to be placed?

The operator pays per search, about $0.023 each; fixed running cost is zero. No paid placement, no affiliate links, no advertising. Public cost ledger

At step 2, before you give a zip

WhoLearns

Cloudflare, GitHub PagesOrdinary request records, with IP address
Anyone elseNothing. The page fetches from no other party

WhatHow long

No accounts, cookies, analytics or tracking.

At step 3, while you wait

How long, and what is it doing?

Median about 3 seconds, slowest about 5. It reads the product, searches, judges which results are shops that sell it, and scores them.

Sells the product0.25Five levels of how sure the site is
