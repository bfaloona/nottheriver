WF1: the decision as a sequence in time

Low-fidelity wireframe. One page is one path of six steps: 1 What, 2 Where, 3 Search, 4 How soon, 5 Which shop, 6 Go. Finished steps fold to one line, the current step is open, and each step carries the questions that arise at that moment ("Asked at this step"). Shops and sources are invented and say "Example". Score values are example values. Underlined text stands for a link; nothing here loads anything. "(o)" stands for the chosen one of a single-choice group, "[ ]" for a check box, "-" for an open question row and "+" for a closed one.

Keyboard and screen reader behavior, the same in every state and at both widths
  • Order: the path is one ordered list. Tab order follows it: folded steps, the open step, then "Asked at this step".
  • Moving on: when a step folds and the next opens, focus moves to the new step's heading, which is read out with its number ("Step 4 of 6").
  • Folded steps: each "change" is a button named with its step ("Change step 1, what you need").
  • Announcements: the wait is announced once; the outcome is announced once as a count ("4 nearby, 1 farther, 4 online") or as the failure message. An error on a field is tied to that field and read when it appears.
  • Question rows: each is a button that says whether its answer is open or closed. Nothing opens on hover.
  • Choices: "How soon" and "Reorder by" are single-choice groups moved through with arrow keys; "only shops with a certification" is a check box.
  • Distance ruler: every bar has its miles and direction in text above it; the bars add nothing a screen reader misses.
  • Motion: none is needed; any that is added is off under a reduced-motion preference.
A static wireframe states this behavior; it cannot prove it.
