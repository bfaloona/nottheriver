# Market position of nottheriver: a blind review

Read 2026-11-11 by one reviewer, from the competitor and list-site research only.

## The answer

- AN1 (novelty is not demand): **The research shows the site is unusual. It does not show that anyone wants it.** The competitor files count which features 48 other sites lack. None of them measures demand, and the synthesis concedes the gap in three places (`research/competitors/README.md:17`, `:22`, `:30`).
- AN2 (real substitutes, my judgment): **For most shoppers the nearest substitute is likely not another ethics tool.** It is Google Maps for a nearby purchase, and "go straight to Target, Etsy, eBay or Bookshop.org" for an online one. Against those, the difference a shopper can see is small: an independent-or-chain badge and a shorter, cleaner list.
- AN3 (builder-facing claims): **Most of the claimed differences are builder-facing.** Per-search cost, open ledger, published accuracy, the budget-spent notice, zip-stays-in-browser and the dispute link are invisible during a shopping task. My judgment: of the 14 compared features, a shopper would clearly notice 3.
- AN4 (thin scores): **The explained ethics score, the feature with the most work behind it, likely shows "nothing found" for most shops** and "caution" for the best-known alternatives. That may read as empty or as nitpicking. Part of the emptiness comes from certifier pages the research could not open (`research/amazon-alternatives.md:178`).
- AN5 (who survived): **Every tool still running has someone paying for it (affiliates, dues, subscriptions, ads or a trade association), and the lasting format is the list article.** Several general-purpose tools without such a line closed or pivoted. The site is general, and no file I read names a way for shoppers to find it.

My recommendation, in one line: narrow to independent shops near the shopper, in a few product categories with a sourced membership roll behind them, and run a head-to-head test against Google Maps before building anything else. If that test fails, the case for stopping the search product and publishing the data instead is strong.

Three points follow, all my judgment.

- **"Without Amazon" does almost no work in a nearby search.** A map of physical shops has almost no Amazon in it to remove. The exception is groceries, where Amazon-owned Whole Foods Market has stores (`research/amazon-alternatives.md:61`). In SH1, the promise in the site's name is invisible; the visible difference is the chain badge.
- **"Without Amazon" does real work only online (SH2, SH4), and there the site competes with a habit that needs no tool**: typing a store name.
- **The site's method marks the shopper's real substitutes as `caution`.** Etsy, eBay, Target, Walmart, Best Buy, Costco and Patagonia all sit there (`research/amazon-alternatives.md:134-150`). A shopper leaving Amazon is likely to read that as "nowhere is good enough", which is a reason to give up, not to switch.

### What a shopper notices is missing

Price, stock, opening hours, reviews and photos. No file shows the site having any of them, and the one stock source found sells shopper location to ad networks (`research/competitors/search-tools.md:122`, `:356`). My judgment: a shopper weighs these absences more heavily than any item in the table above.

### The ethics score is thinnest where the site needs it most

- 21 of the 25 `acceptable` retailers have nothing counted either way; "acceptable" there means "the search found nothing" (`research/amazon-alternatives.md:111`).
- Those are 50 of the most-named national retailers, researched with three concern passes. A single local shop will have less on record. So a nearby result's explanation likely reduces to distance, the chain badge, and "nothing found". This is my inference; the measured check would be the share of the 86 graded nearby shops (`research/competitors/README.md:44`) with any finding or certification.
- The accepted sources (workplace safety citations, labor board cases, California chemical-warning settlements) grow with a company's size and paper trail. Patagonia, with three counted certifications, is `caution`; Etsy is `caution` for goods sold by other sellers; "several of these rest on a single small citation" (`research/amazon-alternatives.md:148-154`, `:174`).
- My judgment: the score partly measures how well documented a company is. A shopper who sees Patagonia flagged and an unknown shop passed may stop trusting the score, and the dispute link does not fix that.

## Where I think the current plan is wrong

- WR1 (position): It treats "no one else combines these" (`research/competitors/README.md:7`) as the position. A combination nobody ships can also be one nobody uses. Google already combines the two parts shoppers use most (DF1, DF2).
- WR2 (order of work): It spends on trust features (DF9 to DF12) before there is evidence that the core result, a shop that sells the thing, beats the substitute.
- WR3 (first line): It leads with what is excluded (Amazon) when the thing delivered is what is included (independent shops nearby). This rests on the site's name and the brief's description; I have not seen the screen (LM1).
- WR4 (reach): No file I read names a way for shoppers to find the site.
